**GPU acceleration** lets Linux apps draw with your device's graphics chip instead of the processor. It's **experimental** and **off by default**.

## Turn it on

Open **Settings › Linux** and switch on **GPU acceleration**. The row shows your device's graphics chip, for example "This device: Adreno 740". It applies to every distro in the app, from the next time a desktop starts.

<Shot name="standalone/settings-linux" alt="Settings, Linux: Run Linux, Android files in Linux, and GPU acceleration marked Experimental" />

## When it helps, and when it doesn't

- **It can make 3D faster on devices with a slower processor**: 3D apps, games and anything that uses OpenGL.
- **On fast devices, software drawing can be quicker.** Try both and keep what feels better.
- **Some apps may glitch, or the desktop may not start.** That's why it's experimental.

Linux draws through VirGL, which passes OpenGL calls to the phone's graphics driver.

## If the desktop stays black

If the desktop is black with GPU acceleration on, Andronix turns it off by itself, restarts the desktop with software drawing, and tells you. The setting then reads "Turned off after a black screen. Turn it on to try again." See [Black screen and safe mode](/beta/troubleshooting/black-screen).

GPU acceleration is never used in safe mode or in the VNC fallback.
