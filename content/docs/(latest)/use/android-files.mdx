Open **Settings › Linux › Android files in Linux** to see how files move between Android and your distro. Nothing leaves your device.

## Shared folders

Two folders are shared between Android and Linux:

| In Linux | On your device |
|---|---|
| `~/Android/Download` | **Download/Andronix** |
| `~/Android/Documents` | **Documents/Andronix** |

What you save in those folders in Linux appears in Android right away, for example in the Files app, a gallery or an email app. Each distro has a `~/Android/README.txt` that says the same.

- **Android 11 and newer:** no permission is needed.
- **Android 8 to 10:** the app asks for the storage permission the first time. If you said no, tap **Allow** on the screen, or allow it in Android's settings.

Only these two folders are shared. The rest of your phone's storage isn't visible inside Linux.

<Callout type="warning">**Android 11 and newer:** files that *other apps* put in Download/Andronix (or Documents/Andronix) aren't visible to Linux, and neither are files from before you reinstalled Andronix. Use **Import** or **Send to Linux** for those (below).</Callout>

## Bring a file into Linux

- **Import:** on the Android files in Linux screen, tap **Import**, then **Photos and videos** or **Files**, and pick what you want.
- **Share:** in any app, share a file and pick **Send to Linux**.

The file is copied to your Linux home: the home of the distro that's running, or the one you used last. The app says when it's done, for example "Copied report.pdf into your Debian home."

## Your Linux home in the Files app

Your Linux home shows up in Android's **Files** app and in file pickers, under **Andronix**, with one entry per distro. You can open, add, rename and delete files there. It only shows the home, not the rest of the distro.

<Callout type="info">**Removing a distro or uninstalling Andronix deletes its Linux home.** Keep copies of anything important in Download/Andronix or Documents/Andronix, or back the distro up first.</Callout>
