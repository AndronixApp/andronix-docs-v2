## The toolbar

A floating toolbar sits over the desktop. Drag its grip to move it.

<Shot name="standalone/desktop-toolbar" alt="The desktop with the toolbar: grip, keyboard, Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt, Shift, arrows, mouse mode, sound, Stop and Hide" />

From left to right:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Move the toolbar** (grip) | Drag to move the toolbar. |
| **Keyboard** | Opens the phone's keyboard, with a row of extra keys on top. |
| **Esc**, **Tab** | Send the key. Hold to repeat. |
| **Ctrl**, **Alt**, **Shift** | Tap: on for the next key. Long-press: locked on. Tap again: off. |
| **Arrows** | Send the arrow keys. Hold to repeat. |
| **Mouse mode** | Switch between Direct touch and Touchpad (below). |
| **Fit to screen** | Shown while you're zoomed in; zooms back out. |
| **Mute** / **Unmute** | Shown when the desktop has sound. |
| **Full screen** / **Exit full screen** | Hides Android's bars and the toolbar. Swipe down from the top edge to see the toolbar for a few seconds. |
| **Desktop size** | Standard, Sharp or Native. See [Tablets and foldables](/desktop/tablets#desktop-size). |
| **Stop** | Stops Linux. |
| **Hide the toolbar** | Collapses it to a small handle; tap **Show the toolbar** to bring it back. |

On a narrow screen the keys scroll; the grip, **Stop** and **Hide the toolbar** always stay visible.

## Typing

Tap **Keyboard**. The phone's keyboard opens with a row of extra keys on top: **Esc**, **Tab**, **Ctrl**, **Alt**, **Shift**, **/**, **-** and the arrows. Gesture typing and autocorrect work in Linux apps.

<Shot name="standalone/extra-keys" alt="The phone keyboard with the extra-key row above it" />

**Copy and paste** work both ways: text you copy in Linux is on Android's clipboard right away. Text you copy in Android reaches Linux when you go back to the desktop.

## Mouse modes

Tap **Mouse mode** to choose:

- **Direct touch** (the default): tap where you want to click. Long-press to right-click.
- **Touchpad**: slide to move the pointer, tap to click, two fingers to scroll or right-click. Double-tap and hold to drag.

<Shot name="standalone/mouse-mode" alt="The Mouse mode picker with Direct touch and Touchpad" />

In both modes: a quick two-finger tap right-clicks, a two-finger drag scrolls, and pinching zooms. The app remembers your choice.

## A hardware keyboard

Connect a Bluetooth or USB keyboard and the toolbar drops the keys you no longer need (Keyboard, Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt, Shift and the arrows).

- **Linux gets every key the app receives**, including F1 to F12, the Meta key (Super in Linux), and Ctrl and Alt combinations.
- **Android keeps a few:** **Back** leaves the desktop (Linux keeps running), and Android's own Home, Recents and system shortcuts never reach the app.
- **Andronix shortcuts:** **Ctrl + Alt + F** switches full screen on or off, and **Ctrl + Alt + Backspace** shows the toolbar in full screen.

<Shot name="standalone/toolbar-hardware-keyboard" alt="The toolbar with a hardware keyboard attached: only mouse mode, sound, full screen, desktop size, Stop and Hide" />

## A mouse

A Bluetooth or USB mouse works as on a computer: moving, left, middle and right click, and the wheel. Over the desktop, Android's pointer hides and Linux draws its own.

In **full screen**, the app captures the mouse, so it moves only inside Linux (handy for games and 3D apps). Press **Ctrl + Alt** to release it; the app says "Mouse released. Click the desktop to capture it again."

## Use the desktop from a computer (VNC)

The app shows the desktop on the phone. To use Linux from a computer on the same Wi-Fi as well, start a second desktop over VNC. In the distro's terminal, run:

```bash copy
vncserver-start 1280x720 2 --lan
```

- **Use display 2.** The app's own desktop uses display 1.
- **Password:** if you haven't set a desktop password yet, it asks for one (6 to 8 characters).
- **Connect:** on the computer, point a VNC viewer at the phone's IP address and display 2, for example `192.168.1.20:2` (port 5902). The IP is in Android's Wi-Fi settings, under the network's details.
- **Sound** from that desktop plays on the phone, not the computer, and only while the app's desktop is running. See [Sound](/use/sound).
- **Stop it** with `vncserver-stop 2`.

<Callout type="warning">With `--lan`, anyone on the same network who knows your desktop password can connect, and VNC traffic isn't encrypted. Use it only on networks you trust, and stop it when you're done.</Callout>
